# VoxCPM2

OpenBMB's tokenizer-free text-to-speech model: speech in 30 languages, Polish included, at 48 kHz. It speaks in a fresh voice, in a voice described in words, in the voice of a short clip, or as a continuation of a clip with its transcript.

## What it can do

- **Speak** (`speak`): `text` in any of the 30 languages (Arabic, Burmese, Chinese, Danish, Dutch, English, Finnish, French, German, Greek, Hebrew, Hindi, Indonesian, Italian, Japanese, Khmer, Korean, Lao, Malay, Norwegian, Polish, Portuguese, Russian, Spanish, Swahili, Swedish, Tagalog, Thai, Turkish, Vietnamese) and nine Chinese dialects, with no language setting.
- **Describe a voice** (`instructions`): gender, age, tone, emotion or pace, e.g. "A calm older man, deep voice". Without a clip it designs a new voice; with a clip it steers the style of the cloned voice. It is sent to the model as the maker's `(description)` prefix.
- **Clone** (`reference.path`, optionally `reference.text`): from the clip alone the model takes its timbre (`clone_mode: reference`). With the clip's exact transcript it continues the clip (`continuation`), and by default does both (`both`, the maker's "ultimate cloning", closest to the speaker).
- **Saved voices** (`clone_voice`, `voices`): a clip with or without its transcript, then `voice` in later requests.
- **Controls**: `cfg_value` (guidance, default 2), `inference_timesteps` (diffusion steps per patch, default 10), `max_tokens` (patch cap per chunk, 6.25 patches per second), `retry_badcase` (up to three attempts per chunk, the seed plus 0, 1, 2, while a chunk runs to its cap without stopping; default on, as in the maker's pipeline), `seed`.

## Usage notes (2 Nov 2026)

What it does: VoxCPM2 reads the text with a MiniCPM-4 language model and writes continuous audio latents one patch (0.16 s) at a time. A residual acoustic model and a small diffusion transformer (ten Euler steps with classifier-free guidance) shape each patch, a stop head ends the utterance, and the AudioVAE V2 decodes the latents straight to 48 kHz. The worker follows the maker's own pipeline: whitespace collapsed, the length capped at 6 patches per text token plus 10, a run that hits the cap tried again with the next seed (three attempts at most), and in continuation modes the last three prompt patches seed the decoder and are cut from the output.

Inputs: text up to 5,000 characters, an estimate of what finishes within one request's 590 s limit (about 35 characters per second in the real check, with a 4× margin), not a measured limit. Text over 400 characters is spoken in sentence groups of at most 400 (cut at spaces, or mid-word in scripts without spaces, when a sentence is longer). Each group is one generation with its own seed, and audio streams per group. With a clip or a saved voice, every group uses that clip. Without one, the groups after the first continue the first: the model reads the first group's words and its own audio as a prompt, as continuation cloning does with a clip and its transcript, and speaks on in that voice. If the first group runs to its length cap without stopping, or a continuation would overflow the context (dense scripts such as Hindi or Thai with long instructions), the later groups speak on their own and may change voice. The groups are joined end to end: no crossfade, and the prosody restarts at each join, which is not the maker's behaviour (it reads a text whole). A "(description)" at the very start of the text, or `instructions`, applies to every group. Reference clips are half a second to 30 seconds and are read at the AudioVAE's 16 kHz. A transcript must match the clip word for word.

Limits: no preset voices and no speed control. The maker's reference denoiser (ZipEnhancer, a separate model) and its text normalizer are not included, so give clean clips and write numbers and abbreviations as words. Voice design and style control vary between runs, as the maker says (set `seed` to repeat a result). Style control on a clone is documented by the maker for a reference clip; with a transcript (continuation) it is untested here. Requests the model cannot serve (a preset voice, a clip under half a second, a transcript without a clip, a prompt and speech that would overflow the 8,192-token context) are refused with 422. `clone_voice` refuses a transcript so long that the saved voice would leave no room for a full group of speech. The maker forbids impersonation, fraud and disinformation and asks that AI speech be labelled.

Source: `mlx-community/VoxCPM2-bf16` at `a7fb80d9` (two safetensors files, 4.96 GB). Its language-model tensors are bit-identical to the maker's `openbmb/VoxCPM2` and its AudioVAE equals the maker's weight-norm-fused FP32 weights. Get casts everything to FP16 (`cast:voxcpm2`, with an FP16 range check), about 4.8 GB on disk. 2.38B parameters, of which the AudioVAE has 94M.

Port: the Swift port from mlx-audio-swift (`e8450402`), checked line by line against the maker's code and corrected. The AudioVAE encoder padded one sample too many in its stride-5 layer, which shifted every reference encoding. Continuation cloning was missing. The prompt text and target are now tokenized as one string, as the maker does. Activations stay in FP16.

Parity: checked against the maker's own code (OpenBMB/VoxCPM `cce58a5`) run once on the Mac's GPU in FP16, AudioVAE included, on a 5 s Polish clip from a macOS voice. All five fixed texts tokenize identically. Relative L2 error: AudioVAE encode 0.47%, decode 0.13%, local encoder 0.10%, language-model prefill 0.14%, diffusion transformer 0.15%, ten flow-matching steps on fixed noise 0.13%. A teacher-forced replay of the maker's 28-step Polish generation (clip plus transcript) kept every stop decision identical, with the worst step within 1.3%, and the 48 kHz decode of the maker's latents was within 0.12%. Bounds were set before the comparison (2% per component, 5% for the replay and the audio). On a 60 s chunk the FP16 AudioVAE decoder's largest activation at any block boundary was 17 (FP16 holds 65,504), and its audio was within 0.48% of an FP32 copy, so the decoder stays FP16. Through the service (results/real-check-2026-11-02.md), Polish and English speech came back word for word when transcribed by Qwen3-ASR-1.7B: without a reference, cloned from a clip with and without its transcript, and with a described voice. An 842-character Polish paragraph (three chunks) and speech in a saved voice came through the service too. Without a clip, the paragraph's chunks were first spoken by different voices (speaker-embedding cosine between the first chunk and the later ones 0.19 to 0.47, against 0.90 to 0.96 between the halves of one chunk, WeSpeaker ResNet34). With the later chunks continuing the first, it was 0.82 to 0.97 on three runs (two seeds, and one described voice), and ASR found no dropped or repeated words. The saved voice was byte-identical to passing its clip inline. The paragraph's few ASR slips ("siódmej", "Mieście") are the model's own: the maker's code reading it in one piece slips the same way. Streamed and file output are byte-identical. Not checked: speed and memory figures, the maker's benchmarks, texts near the 5,000-character cap, real (non-synthetic) reference recordings, languages other than Polish and English.
